# Sword Showdown — Codebase Guide for AI Agents

> **Maintenance rule for AI agents:** If your changes add/remove/rename source files, move logic between modules, introduce new architectural patterns, or add new env vars — update this file and `docs/AI_AGENT_GUIDE.md` in the same commit. Keep the "Common Task Locations" table and directory tree accurate. Stale docs cost more tokens than fresh ones.

## What This Is
A browser-based 3D sword-fighting game, **Sword Showdown**. A visitor who isn't signed in starts on the start screen as a guest; the **sea start** (off for now — `startAsGuest({ seaStart: true })` in `authenticate` turns it back on) puts them out on the sea: standing in a canoe with a fishing rod, only 🎣 Rod / 🛶 Oar / ⬅ Lobby / Sign In on screen, up before the island's mappack has loaded; the canoe points at the island's fishing spot (the player faces over its side until 🛶 Oar), and stepping ashore there starts Town as the guest. The player's phone is the sword controller (gyro + joystick over PeerJS, `public/phone-sword.html`); the desktop/TV browser runs the game. Players fight waves of AI swordsmen, bomb throwers and (later stages) wand casters along a path, each stage ending with a boss that talks first, across the island town map (a map-editor `.mappack` with a pirate ship anchored offshore; Classic keeps the static GLB map), stage by stage, collect coins and, in the village between stages, buy upgrades from the market stall (including a magic wand that casts fire / ice blasts for mana), switch characters, and calibrate the sword before each stage. Once the story's final boss (Pemberton) is beaten, Showdown becomes **open roam**: the player walks the island freely, and each story stage's village has that stage's old boss in it with a quest to take back what Pemberton stole — the stage again, with Pemberton as its final enemy; dying or finishing it goes back to roam. In roam, the anchored pirate ship offers **⛵ Explore the Seas**: it sails out to sea and back with the player kept on deck while frog men climb aboard and attack. **Town** is a living town on the island (Brindlewick, the houses in the north-west): one resident to a house — townsfolk and players together (~18 townsfolk + the player; a player joining a shared town takes a house, a townsperson moving out if none is free) — townsfolk with jobs, homes and needs go about their day on a day / night clock; the player can talk to them and give them coins, or hurt them — a crime someone sees brings wanted stars, guards who fight and carry the player to jail (a cell in the jail house — a home interior with bars and the sheriff's desk; bail, bribe, pick the lock or sleep it off), a sheriff with a bow at ★★★; townsfolk hold fading grudges, call family and the brave for help, brawl and mug each other, and friends follow the player; bandits camp far out of town — each night from 21:00, for three town hours, the sheriff looks for the player to help defend the town — accepted, he leads them to a spot on the Endless walk near where he asked and an Endless wave (not the camp's bandits — just there for the wave) comes at them as they auto-walk a stretch of it, with their own Town gear and no auto-buy, a step harder every third night (`townDefense.js`); held off = no raid tonight + a reward; missed, turned down or lost (going down) = the wave's bandits the player didn't beat raid the town (as many of the gang as that); with no sheriff / no weapon the gang (the camp's own bandits: the ones the player beats stay gone, one more turns up each morning, and a bigger gang carries bombs / shields / bows / wands) raids the town as before (breaking into houses, the player's now and then — taking from its chest —, robbing whoever's out, stripping the fields; a raider who runs into the player fights an armed one and holds up an unarmed one — pay, or run and they fight), more of them join as the days go on, and the morning after the player beats some the sheriff comes over with a reward ; with the sword / wand out the camera auto-aims at whoever's coming at the player — at their camp they warn the player off, then fight an armed one (chasing only off their ground) or rob an unarmed one; after the first day the townsfolk's jobs come in **branches** (`BRANCHES` in `town/quests/jobs.js` — the wizard's, the fisher's; each opens once the first day's done or skipped unless its `from` says a day / hours / after another job), each branch's jobs in order (the next only once the one before is done), the branches side by side but one job at a time: givers waiting to come up queue (a giver near the player first) and none comes while another branch's job is under way or the player's in a fight / out on the water / off the island — talking to any waiting giver (❗) starts theirs; the fisher's first job: bring him 10 wood and he builds the canoe (no canoe in Town till then); his second, the golden fish: he kneels in the canoe's bow with the player, glowing arrows on the water lead to a first spot (a plain fish), then further out to the golden fish's spot (no talk boxes out on the water — he just says his lines; at each spot the canoe stops and the rod comes out, the oar back for the next leg), where it bites — and while it's on the line the pirate ship (the map's own, off its anchor — waiting on the horizon) pulls up, a pirate's bomb rocks the canoe and the fish gets away; he paddles slowly off round a ring of open water with the ship chasing and its crew — bomb throwers whose bombs home in to the player's sword, and archers — attacking one at a time, the player (sword out in the canoe) knocks the bombs and parries the arrows back to kill every pirate, the ship sails off and he paddles home (`townPirates.js`, `beats/sea.js`); each giver coming to find the player like the first day's guides (a ❗ over them meanwhile — 'Later' and they come back; ❓ for a reward), tracked top right with 📍 markers — day 2: the farmer has the cart brought beside the player loaded with veg for the wizard up the mountain, glowing arrows show the drive up, and the wizard pays at his cabin; then he asks for a ride down to the bandit camp to talk to his son Rowan (who doesn't want to be a wizard) — Rowan's bomb knocks the cart over, he runs off and five of his bandit friends attack, the wizard fighting beside the player with his wand (he goes down → the job restarts), then a drive into town — then the bandits' revenge: the wizard's cabin torn down to a heap of dirt and logs (a reusable ruin — `src/environment/ruins.js` + `townRuins.js`), and fifteen logs to chop, load into the cart's bed (the cart holds goods any time — Cart by it) and drive up the mountain; he pays and the cabin's back up by morning; the jobs are quests made of reusable beats (`src/quest/` + `townQuests.js`, data `town/quests/jobs.js`) (skipping the first day hands over a rod and a sword); no other jobs are handed out round town for now; the dead stay dead and newcomers move in; the player's own house is kept for them and bought at its door, with a chest inside for what doesn't fit in the bag (`BAG_MAX` goods) or the wallet (`WALLET_MAX` coins); townsfolk's doors are locked at night (knock: they ask you in, come to the door or don't answer; a job from them or a follower gets you in), other players' homes always, and a bought single-use lock-pick opens any of them (a break-in if seen); in a shared Town (Multiplayer) up to 12 players share one town for good (`townNet.js`): the host's game runs the townsfolk, each player has their own wanted stars, guards chase whoever did it, and others can help — or hunt a wanted player for the bounty. Chickens peck mostly round the farms (a few at a time off on outings anywhere in town / along the roads) and lay eggs; any weapon turns a hen into a drumstick — drumsticks and eggs are picked up, eaten, cooked or sold (at the player's stand or to a farmer) like fish (`townChickens.js`). Its economy runs on wood, fish and veg: each producer sells their own (the fisher fish and fishing rods, the farmer veg and seeds, the woodcutter wood) at a selling spot — one of the two market stalls either side of the shop's door, or one of three spots along each of the shop's side counters — in their selling hours (fish in the morning, veg in the afternoon, so there's always some food), at set prices, and everyone buys food there; the player buys from them (at their spot or by talking to them), sells them their own good back for less, or takes a free spot (Sell here) and, while they stay there, each townsperson who comes up asks to buy something (Sell / No, sorry); the player chops, fishes (with a rod bought from a fisher, held and swung with the gyro like the sword: bubbles rise where the fish are; swing to cast — harder for further, swung across (back-left → out-right / back-right → out-left) to land to that side — and a bobber on the bubbles bites fast with a bigger fish; swipe to hook; the fish swims left / right / out — swipe right / left / up against it; the camera turns once toward where the bobber lands; worn out, the touch reel comes up — the phone's, or the on-screen one without a phone — and cranks it in (the which-way / reel hints only once they've been getting it wrong) — anywhere on foot — the cast goes out in front, landing on water or coming back in (`T.castAhead`)), rides the cart parked by the market (Get in the cart: crouched in its bed, it rolls on the ground where the phone leans — like the broom — anything in hand still usable; Cart: its bed stores up to `CART_MAX` goods from the bag), paddles the canoe moored by the fishing spot once the fisher's built it (standing in it with the oar; the phone's tilt steers it like a joystick: tipped forward it paddles on, back it backs up, leaned left / right it turns that way — the oar's strokes play by themselves to fit) and fishes from it anywhere on the water with the rod, flies the broom bought from the wizard (sitting on it, floating; tilting the phone drifts it that way, it turns toward its drift and the camera swings round behind it) and picks (only their own fields, or a farmer's while helping on the first day), buys the farmers' fields (100 🪙, at the field) to pick from, buys plots of land by the fields (40 🪙 at their signpost — cheaper than a field) and builds up to 3 planter boxes on each (3 wood apiece), plants seeds from the shop only in those boxes (a dirt pile that grows one veg a town day, or in about 40 s once watered with the hose — grabbed at a tap by land the player owns, with empty hands — ✋ Grab / ✋ Drop, like anything grabbable — aimed with the gyro, 💧 Water in place of the Block button), cooks fish, veg, drumsticks and eggs over the campfire (one Cook button → a list like the bag's) into food that heals and sells for more, and trades too, and starts Town with nothing but a few coins (Town's coins, health and gear are its own — Showdown's stay out) and a first day of small guided goals (each one's button ringed like the tutorial's) from the townsfolk — quests made of beats too, like the jobs (`quests/firstDay.js`); townsfolk show how they feel about the player when talked to, remember what the player did (and bring it up), and add up to a town-wide reputation shown in the top bar — even the sword is bought, at the shop (one of the town's houses is the shop — its back counter the weaponsmith's: gear and a mystery chest, shown only while he's in — run by one townsperson, the merchant (role `merchant`, shown as Weaponsmith); its side counters are selling spots, empty unless someone's selling there; the magic wand and the potions are the town wizard's, at his market stall in front of his cabin up the mountain (the Showdown village's stall); walking in through the door puts the player inside the shop room (`shop.glb`), where the things sit on the counters, shopped like the Showdown village's: tap a thing on a counter; townsfolk walk in and out too; every other house is someone's home — walk in through its door into `home_interior.glb`, where its townsfolk go to sleep (seen on the bed), hide, or spend a while; all the rooms sit under the map, out of sight). **Story** is a one-player story mode on Tree Island, saved as it goes (signed in): it opens on a cut scene — black, a lightning flash in the night showing the village boy washed up on a beach of the island's outer coast, black again, then a fade in at sunrise on him sitting on a stone playing a flute, seen from behind — and the **Crow** (a recurring character, `crow.glb`) flies in and asks for the flute: give it (game over, back to the start), throw a rock (a side quest, later) or "What is this place?" — it wants a fish first (the old rod leans in a canoe moored off the beach — the canoe is holed, 3 wood mends it — fish from the shore; bringing the Crow fish is how it gives information and hints); the fish → the Crow tells of a sacred tree inland that can help → the wise tree (`wise_tree.glb`, an old tree with a face in its bark) gives the player a sword → chopping down a tree (Town's tree chopping) and splitting its logs gives wood → the canoe mended → "To be continued…". A frog mom sits on a stone along the way to the sacred tree (no talk yet). The engine (`storyMode.js`) runs a chapter's data (`chapter1.js`), so the story grows by adding data. **Story reuses Town mode's systems as much as possible** (installed on a stand-in for Town's state — see the Story row). **Endless** is Showdown with no end: the player auto-walks every Showdown stage route one after another (starting over on the first after the last) while waves of enemies come at them, each bigger and harder (bomb throwers from wave 2, shield bearers 3, wizards 4, archers 6 — one of a kind the wave it joins, a few more as the waves go on — a champion every 5th wave), until they die (every run starts at level 1 with the base hearts, 0 coins, the sword, the bow (no arrows yet), the magic wand (full mana) and a shield that never breaks; auto-buy still spends what the run earns; the real coins / gear are set aside meanwhile) — the score is the run's kills against their best (kept on the device, so guests and offline play count too, and on the profile when signed in). The game is an installable PWA that plays offline once it has cached itself (everything but Classic's map, Tree Island and the songs). Showdown is single player; **Classic** is a Wii Sports Resort–style take on it (Miis only, swordsmen only, 3 hearts per stage, Block is the only button, minimal HUD). **Multiplayer** mode is a lobby of everyone online where you challenge another player to a 1v1 sword duel in a private room, or start a **Team Battle** (5 v 5) / **Free For All** (10 fighters) with invited players, matchmaking players and bots, each played as **Survival** (last one standing) or **Most Kills** (respawns, most kills when the clock runs out wins); everyone starts with the sword and picks up bows, arrows, shields, bombs, magic wands, mana, hearts, life potions and bubbles scattered around the arena. The old RPG, horde, 3D painter, OSM map and NPC systems were removed. The repo name "qwop-tps" is historical.

## Tech Stack
| Layer | Technology |
|---|---|
| 3D Rendering | Three.js v0.176 (+ `three-mesh-bvh` for map raycasts) |
| Physics | Rapier3D (`@dimforge/rapier3d-compat`) |
| Multiplayer | Firebase Realtime Database (lobby/presence/signaling, shop stock, Town rooms) + PeerJS WebRTC (Multiplayer mode: duel challenges, battle parties/matchmaking, presence, sword state; a shared Town's room; separately the phone controller link) |
| Map | Editor `.mappack` (`public/mappacks/island_town.mappack`, loaded by `src/map/MapLoader.ts` + `jszip`, KTX2 via `public/basis/`) for every mode but Classic; Classic = static GLB (`public/glb_map/map.glb`); height via BVH raycast |
| Build tool | Vite 6 |
| Deploy | Vercel (with `/api/turn-credentials` serverless function) |
| Auth | PIN-based (SHA-256 hashed, stored in Firebase + cookie); guest play saves nothing |

## Key Architectural Patterns

### 1. `appContext` — Central Dependency Injection
Shared runtime state lives in **`src/core/appContext.js`** in typed buckets:
```js
appContext.entities   // otherPlayers, weapons
appContext.systems    // playerControls, rapierWorld, rbToMesh
appContext.uiState    // appState
appContext.settings   // user preferences, startupPhases
appContext.debugFlags // PERF, DEBUG_CONSOLE
```
`exposeDebugGlobals.js` mirrors these onto `window.*` (compat shims + dev console). New code should go through `appContext` rather than adding raw globals.

### 2. Feature Facade Pattern (`src/features/`)
Files in `features/` are **thin re-export + lazy-load wrappers** to enable Vite code splitting. `combatFeature.js` re-exports `projectiles.js` and lazy-loads the weapon modules (shield, pistol, foam sword); `uiPanelsFeature.js` lazy-loads the shop. When adding new heavy features, add a facade here.

### 3. Multiplayer: lobby + duel rooms (Multiplayer mode only)
- Peer multiplayer only exists in Multiplayer mode and a shared Town: `startMultiplayer(roomId)` / `stopMultiplayer` in `bootstrapGameApp.js` create/destroy the `Multiplayer` instance (Showdown and the tutorial are single player; `multiplayer` is `null` there — guard every use). Town: `townNet` (`src/town/townNet.js`) starts it in room `town-<roomId>` and stops it on leaving Town (duelMode's delayed stop skips while `townNet.isOn()`); `_netRecipients` / presence / `_getPvpTargets` include the town's players; message type `town`
- Firebase Realtime Database = `peers/<id>` ({name, roomId}) lists everyone online (the lobby list), `rooms/<roomId>/<id>` drives who connects to whom; the shop stock is global (`merchantInventory`)
- Rooms: everyone starts in `lobby`; a duel moves both players to `duel-<challengeId>` (`joinRoom`) and back afterwards. Battles: a solo party host waits in `mm-<mode>-<rule>` (the matchmaking queue), a party with guests in `party-<hostId>`, the battle itself in `match-<matchId>`
- PeerJS WebRTC message types: `presence` (position/animation/equipment), `projectile`, `duel` (`op`: challenge/accept/decline/cancel/state/hit/blocked/dead/leave — see `duelMode.js`), `match` (party/queue/battle ops — see the header of `matchMode.js`). Game traffic is sent only to the duel opponent / the other battle players (`sendTo`, `_netRecipients`); presence from anyone else is ignored
- Battles: the host (whoever pressed Start) simulates the bots, owns the pickups (`take` → `taken`) and the score (`dead {by}` → `kill {v, by, sc}`), and decides the winner; bot state is broadcast (`bots`), humans hit bots via `botHit` to the host. The host leaving ends the battle
- Duel hits: the attacker's sword check detects the hit and sends `hit`; the victim applies the damage and sends `dead` when it dies
- One peer per room elected "host"; others connect to it (star)
- Topology mode configurable via `VITE_NETWORK_TOPOLOGY_MODE` env var
- The phone controller has its own PeerJS connection to the game (`_attachPhoneSwordConn` in `bootstrapGameApp.js`; the newest phone connection replaces an older one — `_phoneSwordConn`); TURN servers come from `/api/turn-credentials`. The game's gyro peer (`_createGyroPeer`) stays registered on the signalling server so a phone can reconnect mid-game: it reconnects on `disconnected` (backoff, and at once on `visibilitychange` / `online` / `focus`), is recreated when destroyed, and retries its fixed ID while a stale registration holds it (`_fixedPeerId` = a hash of `profileNameKey` — one phone URL per profile — or, for guests, of a per-browser device ID kept in localStorage + cookie `sq:guestDeviceId`, so the URL survives refreshes); the phone page retries `peer-unavailable` / times out a connection that never opens (`connectToGame` in `public/phone-sword.html`)

### 4. World: maps per mode
`MAPS` in `bootstrapGameApp.js` lists the maps: `islandTown` (`public/mappacks/island_town.mappack`, loaded with `MapLoader` from `src/map/MapLoader.ts` — the map editor's runtime, edited for the game: `userData.mapObject` on placed objects, each placed object wrapped in a group so the `normalisePivot` offset survives (the editor's copy overwrites it, misplacing GLBs whose origin isn't at their bottom-centre), KTX2 loader disposed, fetch errors thrown, model files loaded in parallel, the meshopt decoder set on its GLTFLoader (packs run through `scripts/optimize-mappack.mjs`), the `sea: false` load option (no ocean plane, a small seabed apron — `SEA_APRON_EXTENT`); replace it with a new editor copy and re-apply those; run every exported pack through `npm run optimize:mappack -- <pack>` — WebP model textures + meshopt, ~2.5× smaller) (scaled ×1.4 by its `scale` — spawn, `DUEL_LOCATION` / `ARENA_LOCATION` and `STAGE_ROUTES` are in scaled world units; the water sampler un-scales its queries) for Showdown, the tutorial, Town and Multiplayer, and `classic` (`public/glb_map/map.glb`, ×5) for Classic (`mapForMode`). The default map loads at boot; the mode handler (`setModeHandler`) awaits `_setActiveMap(mapForMode(mode))` before starting a mode — it loads a map the first time (cached after), swaps it into the scene, clears the height cache, re-registers the bullet meshes (`registerMapMeshes`) and the spawn centre (`setSpawnCenter`), and a changed map teleports the player to its spawn. Each map's meshes get a `three-mesh-bvh` BVH and one downward-raycast height resolver (`registerTerrainHeightResolver`, `src/environment/terrainHeight.js`) casts against the active map's ground meshes (cached on a 0.5 m grid — `_glbCellLayers` — and blended bilinearly between the 4 grid points round x, z — `_glbLayers`, edges over `_GLB_BLEND_MAX_STEP` kept sharp — so slopes have no stair-steps; the player's small step ups / downs are eased too — `GROUND_EASE_*` in `controls.js`). Everything that needs ground height (`getTerrainHeight`, `getSpawnY`) goes through that resolver. Mappack grass / scatter (instanced) is neither ground nor solid; objects matching a map's `noGround` (trees) and the water meshes (`MAP_NOT_GROUND`: `sea` / `seabed` / `water`) stop bullets but aren't stood on. Trees' trunks are solid instead: `_measureMapTrunks` (in `_setActiveMap`, one `measureTrunk` per tree model, carried over to its copies) → `setMapObstacles` (`src/environment/obstacles.js` — the player slides round them in `PlayerControls._walkStep` / is pushed out in `processMovement`) + a Rapier cylinder per trunk for enemies' bodies (`_syncTrunkColliders`); the leaves stay walk-through. The map editor's per-model collider setting isn't exported to the `.mappack` and isn't read. A pack with a sea level (the island) sets the ground floor `SEA_WADE_DEPTH` under it (`setTerrainFloor`; the flat Rapier ground collider's top follows it too — `_syncPhysicsGround` — so enemies' dynamic bodies can go below y = 0), so characters wade in the sea instead of sinking; `_isOverWater(x, z)` (MapLoader's `sampleMapWaterLevel`, bridges count as dry) makes `_psPathSteepness` steer stage paths away from water (`PS_PATH_WATER_PENALTY`). The first map loaded is the art-style reference (its objects' textures; KTX2 textures are skipped by `artStyle.js`). Multiplayer spots (`DUEL_LOCATION`, `ARENA_LOCATION`) are island town coordinates. A mappack with a sea can carry `floating` GLB props (the island's pirate ship): loaded in `_loadMap` (`_loadFloatingProp`, pack units, origin on the sea surface), stylized, bobbing on the swell (the map's `animate(t)`, called each frame — `FLOAT_*`); they stop bullets and are kept out of the map's height raycast (`noGround` — their masts would be the ground), but one with `deckMaxY` (the ship: 1.5 model units) is ground of its own: `_floatingGroundHeight` (a second, uncached height resolver — it bobs) casts down onto its highest upward-facing surface no higher than `deckMaxY` (the deck — masts / yards / sails ignored), so it is climbed from the water like any wall and stood on. One with a `deck` rect (the ship) can sail: open roam's voyage moves its `holder` (`_voyageUpdate`, see Common Task Locations) and moves it back to its `anchor` afterwards. A map's `islands` (Tree Island) are other mappacks in the same world, out on its sea — streamed in from Town's canoe, no map switch (see the Islands row in Common Task Locations).

### 5. Stages (`_ps*` in `bootstrapGameApp.js`)
Between stages the player is in the **village** (`src/village/villageMode.js`, see Common Task Locations), built around wherever they stand (or at the stage route's first point); the villager stands on the next stage's path and gives a quest for it (accepting starts the stage). A stage with a hand-authored route (`STAGE_ROUTES` in `src/map/stageRoutes.js`, recorded with Settings → Dev → Free Roam) auto-walks along its waypoints (following the line, not touching each point — see Common Task Locations); otherwise it picks the flattest direction from the start (`_psPickPathAngle` — picked when the village opens and passed to `_psStartStage` / `_psBuildStage`). `_psBuildStage` places swordsmen, bombers and coins along it (`_psBuildStage`; from the village they start `PS_VILLAGE_EXIT_DIST` m out so the player walks out first), then auto-walks the player between fights. Enemies (`EnemyPlayer`, `BombThrowerEnemy`) live in the `hordeEnemies` array (historical name).

### 6. GLB Character + IK Arms
Players render `public/models/glb_characters/village_boy.glb` (`glbCharacterConfig.villageBoyUrl`) by default (Showdown lets them pick an unlocked character, Multiplayer battles a roster character); Stage enemies' characters are preloaded in the village (`preloadCharacterModel`) and a queued enemy only spawns once its model is in (`isCharacterModelSettled`), so none fights invisible. EnemyPlayers without a `characterUrl` cycle through `pumpkin.glb`, `antler_guy.glb` (`glbCharacterConfig.antlerGuyUrl`), `frog_man.glb` (`glbCharacterConfig.frogManUrl`), `gemhorn_rigged.glb` (`glbCharacterConfig.url`, the default) `wizard.glb` (`glbCharacterConfig.wizardUrl`) and `tree_creature.glb` (`glbCharacterConfig.treeCreatureUrl`) per spawn (Showdown stages pass one) — all share the Mixamo skeleton, picked with the `url` option of `createGLBCharacterInstance`. No character has shell fur any more (`glbCharacterConfig.fluffy.shells` = 0 — bouncy secondary motion only); per-model fluffy overrides go in `glbCharacterConfig.fluffyByUrl`. Every model is scaled to the same `targetHeight` from its bind-pose bounds; per-model size multipliers go in `glbCharacterConfig.heightScaleByUrl` (tree creature and gemhorn 1.4×, villager (Pemberton) 1.1×, village boy / girl 0.8×; a smaller character also gets a lower player camera — `cameraHeightByUrl` → `characterCameraHeight`, used by `PlayerControls`: village boy 0.9×). `fluffyCharacter.ts` retargets the Mixamo clips (walk/run/idle — all in `public/models/animations/animations.glb`, one action per clip, addressed `animations.glb#<name>`) onto it and adds fur; a moving character plays the `Slow Run` clip (`runClip`) instead of the walk once its ground speed — measured from its world position in `GLBCharacter.animate` (`_updateSpeed`), so the same for the player, remote players and enemies — passes `glbCharacterConfig.runSpeed` (3 m/s; back to the walk below `runSpeed × runStopRatio`); the clip drives everything **except** the arm chains. Arms are posed each frame by a stretchy two-bone IK (`GLBCharacter.solveArm`) toward invisible floating-hand groups, which are also the weapon attach points (`userData.proceduralHand` markers, preferred by `Weapon._getHandBone`). The GLB and clips face +Z (game forward) — no Y180 needed. Hand labels are mirrored: the `'right'` floating hand is at local +X, i.e. the anatomical left arm. Per-character weapon hold: `glbCharacterConfig.holdByUrl` (`characterHold(url)` → `{ grip, sword }`) scales the floating-hand grip targets in `updateProceduralPlayerRig` / `updateRemotePlayerRig` and the held sword (`FoamSword.update`, `createSwordMesh`) — village boy grips at 0.76 with a 0.8 sword. Which arms the IK drives is per hand (`GLBCharacter.setArmIKHands({ left, right })`, the clip animates the others and restarts to pick them up): the local player with nothing equipped (✋ Hands, the village…) has both arms on the clip — the gyro doesn't move an empty hand; only while something held asks for it (Town's hose — `userData.gyroHandAt`, refreshed each frame) the anatomical right arm (`'left'` hand) goes on the IK, following the phone gyro (`userData.gyroArmQuat` / `gyroArmTime`, set in the phone-sword gyro loop of `bootstrapGameApp.js`, `GYRO_ARM_*` in `playerModel.js`). A held pose: `GLBCharacter.playPose(url)` loops a clip over the whole body (IK suspended) until `endPose()` — Town's broom sits the player down with `sitClip` (the `Sitting` clip), Town's cart crouches them with `crouchClip` (the `Crouch` clip), wall climbing loops `climbClip` (the `Climb` clip); `playPose(url, { armIK: true })` keeps the IK arms on the IK (Story: sitting on the stone holding the flute; the broom: whatever is in hand). On death, `GLBCharacter.playDeath()` plays the `Flying Back Death` clip once over the whole body (IK suspended, hands follow the palms) until `revive()`; EnemyPlayer keeps its ragdoll during it, with knockback capped by `DEATH_KNOCKBACK_CAP`. Bomb blasts reuse the clip without dying: `EnemyPlayer.applyBlastKnockback()` (ragdoll + `playDeath()`, `revive()` in `_endRagdoll`, force/cap `BLAST_KNOCKBACK`) and `_blastPlayer()` in `bootstrapGameApp.js` for the local player. `BombThrowerEnemy` uses the same GLB with `armIK: false` (clips drive the arms, no floating hands): `playAction(glbCharacterConfig.throwClip)` plays the `Throw` clip once and releases the bomb at `THROW_RELEASE_AT` of the clip; between throws a bomb mesh is snapped to the anatomical right palm (`getPalmWorldPosition('left')` — labels are mirrored). Blasts from other throwers play the death clip for `BLAST_STUN_MS`, then `revive()`.

**Mii characters** (`mii1.glb`, later `mii2.glb`… — `isMiiCharacterUrl` matches `/miiN.glb`) have no shoulders, arms or hands. `_buildArm` finds no chains, so `solveArm` leaves the floating hands where the game put them and `getPalmWorldPosition` returns a point beside the body (`palmFallback`, bomber / player bomb). Their hands are drawn by the sword: a Mii holds the Wii sword (`wii_sword.glb`, `SWORD_VARIANTS.wii` in `swordModel.js`) whose instances carry two brown ball hands on the handle. `createSwordModelInstance({ characterUrl })` picks the variant (`swordVariantForCharacter`); `FoamSword` swaps its model when the player's character changes, `EnemyPlayer._buildSword` uses the enemy's character, and `createSwordMesh(characterUrl)` (duel / battle opponents) the opponent's. Miis are Classic only. New Mii: add the GLB, a `glbCharacterConfig` url and add it to `CLASSIC_MII_URLS` (not `MATCH_CHARACTERS`).

**Unrigged characters** (no skeleton — `villager.glb` for now) load through the same `createGLBCharacterInstance`, but `GLBCharacter` gets a `StaticRig` instead of the `FluffyCharacter`: no clips or arm IK, just procedural motion (breathing idle, a walking bob, tipping over on death) and `palmFallback` hands. Rig the GLB (Mixamo skeleton) and it uses the normal path automatically.

### 7. Start screen + tutorial
After login the start screen shows only **Start Game** (runs the tutorial) until the profile has `tutorialCompleted: true`; after that it shows **Tutorial**, **Story**, **Showdown**, **Classic**, **Town** and **Multiplayer**. Auth resolves as soon as the player is logged in, so the game (and the settings panel) loads behind the start screen; a mode picked before it's ready is queued and started when `setModeHandler` is set (the auto phone-QR popup is deferred until a mode is picked, `autoShowPhoneSwordQr`). When the tutorial finishes, `arcadeOverlay.showStartScreen()` brings the start screen back and the mode handler starts the next mode in the running game. The ⚙️ settings button (`#settings-button`) sits above the start screen, the stage overlay and the Multiplayer lobby (z-index in `styles.css`) and stays visible in every mode (Classic included), hidden until `body.settings-ready` (set once `initSettingsPanel` has run — the default map loads in parallel, `_defaultMapReady`, and the boot never waits on it: the game loop starts without it (the guest's sea start plays meanwhile), a picked mode waits on it in the mode handler, and the player is put on the map's ground at the spawn once it's in unless out on the sea). While a mode is running the settings panel shows a **⬅ Back to Lobby** footer button (`buildFooter` in `settingsPanel.js`) → `appState.returnToLobby` (stops the tutorial via `showdownTutorial.stop()`, exits Multiplayer / Classic, `_resetForMenu`, `showStartScreen`). The tutorial script (`src/tutorial/showdownTutorial.js`) drives enemies through `EnemyPlayer.script` (`passive` / `block` / `windup`) and the bomber's `throwsHeld` / `aimAt`, and touches the game only through `tutorialCtx` in `bootstrapGameApp.js`. While it runs, health never drops (`localHealth` setter), auto-buy is paused (it buys scripted), and tutorial enemies drop no coins and don't count as kills (`_tutorial`).

### 8. UI design system (`styles.css`)
Every screen — start screen, settings / shop / leaderboard (`.settings-shell`), village panels / buy card, phone QR / calibration popups, Multiplayer lobby / battles, tutorial, game over, HUD — shares one dark glass look. The top of `styles.css` holds the **tokens** on `:root` (`--font-ui` Inter / `--font-display` Outfit, loaded from Google Fonts in `index.html`; `--surface*`, `--border*`, `--text*`, gold `--accent*`, status colours, `--radius-*`, `--shadow-*`, `--menu-bg` for full-screen menus, `--scrim` for modal backdrops) and three **shared components** applied by grouped selectors: **panel** (`.ui-panel`, `.arcade-shell`, `.settings-shell`, `.duel-lobby-panel`, phone popups…), **button** — gold primary (`.ui-btn`, `.arcade-button`, `.settings-button`…), glass secondary (`.ui-btn-secondary`, `.arcade-secondary`, …) and ghost (`.ui-btn-ghost`, `.arcade-guest`, back buttons) — and **chip** (`.ui-chip`, settings / leaderboard tabs; selected = `.is-active` / `.active`). New UI: use the `ui-*` classes (or add the new class to the matching group) and the tokens, not new colours / fonts. HUD elements are `--surface-hud` pills without `backdrop-filter` (it would re-blur the 3D view every frame). Classic keeps its Wii-style lettering (stage banner, Ready/Go!, combo) but uses the shared panel/buttons. `public/phone-sword.html` repeats the tokens it needs in its own `:root`.

**Icons in place of emoji:** `public/assets/ui/icons/pixelIcons.js` (a plain script in `index.html` and `public/phone-sword.html`, before the app) maps emoji → a cell of one of two sheets (`SHEETS`): `pixel-icons.webp` (pixel art, lossless — `PIXEL`, [row, column], drawn pixelated) and `painted-icons.webp` (painted, one atlas of the two painted packs — `PAINTED`, wins where both have one: ❗ ❓ 📍, coin, heart, sword, shield, chest, backpack, fire, canoe, gun, house, lock, bullets, the characters' portraits, mood faces, ★ / ☆, the bell…; each pack cut from its dark background — blobs by distance from the background colour, flood-filled from the edges + enclosed holes, edges feathered, packed at 64 px, WebP q80). A MutationObserver wraps every mapped emoji in the DOM in a `<px-icon>` (the emoji stays as its transparent text, so `textContent` is unchanged), so UI code keeps writing plain emoji; canvas text goes through `window.pixelIcons.drawText` / `measureText` (`makeTextSprite` in `villageMode.js` — 3D signs / markers). An emoji with no icon in `ICONS` stays an emoji; to add one, add its cell. Direction: minimal on-screen UI and messages (Town talk = subtitles, no name tags over townsfolk). **Emoji only where it is the content**: item / goods / character icon fields (`emoji:`), counters (coins, bag, bombs, bubbles, kills, mana), prices next to a number, ❗ / ❓ / 📍 markers, ★ stars and icon-only buttons; button labels, feed lines, toasts, banners, speech and tutorial text are plain words ("+3 coins", "Shop", "Get out"). Button labels quoted elsewhere in this guide may still show the emoji they used to carry.

### 9. PIN Auth
No OAuth. Player registers with name + numeric PIN. PIN is `SALT + SHA-256` hashed client-side via Web Crypto, stored in Firebase. Hash cached in cookie for auto-login.

**Guest play:** the app opens as a guest on the start screen (`startAsGuest` in `createArcadeOverlay`; the sea start — `startAsGuest({ seaStart: true })` — is off) unless a signed-in player is remembered (stored PIN → auto-login). The start screen's "Sign In" / "Switch user" button (`data-arcade-switch`, kept visible when returning from any mode via `showStartScreen`) reloads the page with the `sq:showLogin` sessionStorage flag, which opens the login form instead; "Play Without Signing In" there (`data-arcade-guest`) goes back to guest. Guest play skips auth with a random name (`randomGuestName`) and an in-memory profile (`buildGuestProfile`, all modes offered). `initCore` then has `isGuest` true and `profileNameKey` null — every profile save (`saveStats*` wrappers, stage/characters/tutorial/phoneSwordStats, localStorage `ps_*` keys) is skipped when the key is null, `playerName` isn't stored, and renaming only changes the session name. Guard new profile writes on `profileNameKey`.

## Build & Dev


Vite splits vendor chunks: `vendor-three`, `vendor-rapier`, `vendor-firebase`.

### Headless browser testing (Playwright / the pre-installed Chromium)

- **No phone popup under automation:** the phone link / QR popup never comes up by itself when `navigator.webdriver` is true (Playwright) or the URL has `?noPhonePopup` (`PHONE_SWORD_AUTO_POPUP` in `bootstrapGameApp.js`), so screenshots aren't covered by it — don't dismiss it. Add `?phonePopup` to test the popup itself (Settings → Show Phone QR Code opens it either way).
- **Known artifact — ignore it:** in headless Chromium (SwiftShader software GL) the skybox renders as big flat **dark-blue and grey triangles** across the sky when the camera faces some directions. It happens with every scene object hidden and at the island's spawn too, so it is the headless renderer, not the map, a mesh or your change. Don't debug it.
- **Build without a `.env`:** Firebase throws at boot without a database URL. Build with fake values and serve `dist/` statically: `VITE_FIREBASE_DATABASE_URL=https://fake-test-default-rtdb.firebaseio.com VITE_FIREBASE_PROJECT_ID=fake-test VITE_FIREBASE_API_KEY=x VITE_FIREBASE_APP_ID=1:1:web:1 npx vite build --outDir <scratch>/dist`, then `python3 -m http.server 4173 -d <scratch>/dist`. Network errors (Firebase, PeerJS CDN) in the console are expected; Town falls back to "just you". Launch with `--use-gl=swiftshader --enable-unsafe-swiftshader`.
- **Getting into Town:** the page opens as a guest on the start screen; click `[data-arcade-town]` (or, with the sea start turned back on, set `seaStart.debug().boat` to `x = -71, z = -102` until `seaStart.interactLabel() === 'Get out'`, then `seaStart.interact()`); wait for `town.isActive() && town.debug().T.ready`. Then `town.debug().ctx.teleport(x, z, yaw)` moves the player, `town.debug().T.acts` lists the interact buttons (`a.onClick()`), and `window.playerControls.yaw` turns the camera (set it every frame for a while — other code resets it).
- Software rendering is slow: loads that take a second on a GPU (a mappack, a big GLB) can take 20–30 s, so wait on a condition, not a fixed sleep.
