## What This Is
A browser-based 3D sword-fighting game, **Sword Showdown**. A visitor who isn't signed in starts out on the sea (the **sea start**): standing in a canoe with a fishing rod, only 🎣 Rod / 🛶 Oar / ⬅ Lobby / Sign In on screen, up before the island's mappack has loaded; the canoe points at the island's fishing spot (the player faces over its side until 🛶 Oar), and stepping ashore there starts Town as the guest. The player's phone is the sword controller (gyro + joystick over PeerJS, `public/phone-sword.html`); the desktop/TV browser runs the game. Players fight waves of AI swordsmen, bomb throwers and (later stages) wand casters along a path, each stage ending with a boss that talks first, across the island town map (a map-editor `.mappack` with a pirate ship anchored offshore; Classic keeps the static GLB map), stage by stage, collect coins and, in the village between stages, buy upgrades from the market stall (including a magic wand that casts fire / ice blasts for mana), switch characters, and calibrate the sword before each stage. Once the story's final boss (Pemberton) is beaten, Showdown becomes **open roam**: the player walks the island freely, and each story stage's village has that stage's old boss in it with a quest to take back what Pemberton stole — the stage again, with Pemberton as its final enemy; dying or finishing it goes back to roam. In roam, the anchored pirate ship offers **⛵ Explore the Seas**: it sails out to sea and back with the player kept on deck while frog men climb aboard and attack. **Town** is a living town on the island (Brindlewick, the houses in the north-west): one resident to a house — townsfolk and players together (~18 townsfolk + the player; a player joining a shared town takes a house, a townsperson moving out if none is free) — townsfolk with jobs, homes and needs go about their day on a day / night clock; the player can talk to them and give them coins, or hurt them — a crime someone sees brings wanted stars, guards who fight and carry the player to jail (a cell in the jail house — a home interior with bars and the sheriff's desk; bail, bribe, pick the lock or sleep it off), a sheriff with a gun at ★★★; townsfolk hold fading grudges, call family and the brave for help, brawl and mug each other, and friends follow the player; bandits camp far out of town and carry bounties — every night the gang (the camp's own bandits: the ones the player beats stay gone, one more turns up each morning, and a bigger gang carries bombs / shields / guns / wands) raids the town (breaking into houses, the player's now and then — taking from its chest —, robbing whoever's out, stripping the fields; a raider who runs into the player fights an armed one and holds up an unarmed one — pay, or run and they fight), more of them join as the days go on, and the morning after the player beats some the sheriff comes over with a reward (some days he asks an armed player to stand watch — a job); with the sword / wand out the camera auto-aims at whoever's coming at the player — at their camp they warn the player off, then fight an armed one (chasing only off their ground) or rob an unarmed one; townsfolk hand out jobs (a ❗ over whoever has one, like the Showdown villager; talk to take it, back to them — ❓ — for the reward) made from the town's real needs plus authored quest chains, tracked top right with 📍 markers; the dead stay dead and newcomers move in; the player's own house is kept for them and bought at its door, with a chest inside for what doesn't fit in the bag (`BAG_MAX` goods) or the wallet (`WALLET_MAX` coins); townsfolk's doors are locked at night (knock: they ask you in, come to the door or don't answer; a job from them or a follower gets you in), other players' homes always, and a bought single-use lock-pick opens any of them (a break-in if seen); in a shared Town (Multiplayer) up to 12 players share one town for good (`townNet.js`): the host's game runs the townsfolk, each player has their own wanted stars, guards chase whoever did it, and others can help — or hunt a wanted player for the bounty. Its economy runs on wood, fish and veg: producers sell to the market, everyone buys food there, prices follow the stock; the player chops, fishes (with a rod bought at the shop, held and swung with the gyro like the sword: swing to cast, pull back to hook, pull when the fish tires, crank the phone's reel to land it — at the town's one fishing spot), paddles the canoe moored by the fishing spot (standing in it with the oar; tilting the phone's top to the left dips the oar into the water on the left, beside them — to the right on the right —, then tilting the top toward them sweeps it back — a forward stroke, turning a little the other way — or away from them sweeps it ahead — a back stroke, weaker; straightening it lifts the oar out, and the next stroke starts once the phone's back at the middle) and fishes from it anywhere on the water with the rod, flies the broom bought from the wizard (sitting on it, floating; tilting the phone drifts it that way, it turns toward its drift and the camera swings round behind it) and picks (or steals), buys the farmers' fields (100 🪙, at the field) to pick from, plants seeds from the shop anywhere (a dirt pile that grows one veg a town day, or in about 40 s once watered with the hose — grabbed at its tap with empty hands — ✋ Grab / ✋ Drop, like anything grabbable — aimed with the gyro, 💧 Water in place of the Block button), cooks fish and veg over the campfire into food that heals and sells for more, and trades too, and starts Town with nothing but a few coins (Town's coins, health and gear are its own — Showdown's stay out) and a first day of small guided goals from the townsfolk; townsfolk show how they feel about the player when talked to, remember what the player did (and bring it up), and add up to a town-wide reputation shown in the top bar — even the sword is bought, at the shop (one of the town's houses is the shop, which sells everything — goods, seeds, gear and a mystery chest — run by one townsperson, the merchant; the magic wand and the potions are the town wizard's, at his market stall out in the woods (the Showdown village's stall); walking in through the door puts the player inside the shop room (`shop.glb`), where the things sit on the counters, shopped like the Showdown village's: tap a thing on a counter; townsfolk walk in and out too; every other house is someone's home — walk in through its door into `home_interior.glb`, where its townsfolk go to sleep (seen on the bed), hide, or spend a while; all the rooms sit under the map, out of sight). Showdown is single player; **Classic** is a Wii Sports Resort–style take on it (Miis only, swordsmen only, 3 hearts per stage, Block is the only button, minimal HUD). **Multiplayer** mode is a lobby of everyone online where you challenge another player to a 1v1 sword duel in a private room, or start a **Team Battle** (5 v 5) / **Free For All** (10 fighters) with invited players, matchmaking players and bots, each played as **Survival** (last one standing) or **Most Kills** (respawns, most kills when the clock runs out wins); everyone starts with the sword and picks up guns, bullets, shields, bombs, magic wands, mana, hearts, life potions and bubbles scattered around the arena. The old RPG, horde, 3D painter, OSM map and NPC systems were removed. The repo name "qwop-tps" is historical.

## Tech Stack
| Layer | Technology |
|---|---|
| 3D Rendering | Three.js v0.176 (+ `three-mesh-bvh` for map raycasts) |
| Physics | Rapier3D (`@dimforge/rapier3d-compat`) |
| Multiplayer | Firebase Realtime Database (lobby/presence/signaling, shop stock, Town rooms) + PeerJS WebRTC (Multiplayer mode: duel challenges, battle parties/matchmaking, presence, sword state; a shared Town's room; separately the phone controller link) |
| Map | Editor `.mappack` (`public/mappacks/island_town.mappack`, loaded by `src/map/MapLoader.ts` + `jszip`, KTX2 via `public/basis/`) for every mode but Classic; Classic = static GLB (`public/glb_map/map.glb`); height via BVH raycast |
| Build tool | Vite 6 |
| Deploy | Vercel (with `/api/turn-credentials` serverless function) |
| Auth | PIN-based (SHA-256 hashed, stored in Firebase + cookie); guest play saves nothing |

## Key Architectural Patterns

### 1. `appContext` — Central Dependency Injection
Shared runtime state lives in **`src/core/appContext.js`** in typed buckets:
```js
appContext.entities   // otherPlayers, weapons
appContext.systems    // playerControls, rapierWorld, rbToMesh
appContext.uiState    // appState
appContext.settings   // user preferences, startupPhases
appContext.debugFlags // PERF, DEBUG_CONSOLE
```
`exposeDebugGlobals.js` mirrors these onto `window.*` (compat shims + dev console). New code should go through `appContext` rather than adding raw globals.

### 2. Feature Facade Pattern (`src/features/`)
Files in `features/` are **thin re-export + lazy-load wrappers** to enable Vite code splitting. `combatFeature.js` re-exports `projectiles.js` and lazy-loads the weapon modules (shield, pistol, foam sword); `uiPanelsFeature.js` lazy-loads the shop. When adding new heavy features, add a facade here.

### 3. Multiplayer: lobby + duel rooms (Multiplayer mode only)
- Peer multiplayer only exists in Multiplayer mode and a shared Town: `startMultiplayer(roomId)` / `stopMultiplayer` in `bootstrapGameApp.js` create/destroy the `Multiplayer` instance (Showdown and the tutorial are single player; `multiplayer` is `null` there — guard every use). Town: `townNet` (`src/town/townNet.js`) starts it in room `town-<roomId>` and stops it on leaving Town (duelMode's delayed stop skips while `townNet.isOn()`); `_netRecipients` / presence / `_getPvpTargets` include the town's players; message type `town`
- Firebase Realtime Database = `peers/<id>` ({name, roomId}) lists everyone online (the lobby list), `rooms/<roomId>/<id>` drives who connects to whom; the shop stock is global (`merchantInventory`)
- Rooms: everyone starts in `lobby`; a duel moves both players to `duel-<challengeId>` (`joinRoom`) and back afterwards. Battles: a solo party host waits in `mm-<mode>-<rule>` (the matchmaking queue), a party with guests in `party-<hostId>`, the battle itself in `match-<matchId>`
- PeerJS WebRTC message types: `presence` (position/animation/equipment), `projectile`, `duel` (`op`: challenge/accept/decline/cancel/state/hit/blocked/dead/leave — see `duelMode.js`), `match` (party/queue/battle ops — see the header of `matchMode.js`). Game traffic is sent only to the duel opponent / the other battle players (`sendTo`, `_netRecipients`); presence from anyone else is ignored
- Battles: the host (whoever pressed Start) simulates the bots, owns the pickups (`take` → `taken`) and the score (`dead {by}` → `kill {v, by, sc}`), and decides the winner; bot state is broadcast (`bots`), humans hit bots via `botHit` to the host. The host leaving ends the battle
- Duel hits: the attacker's sword check detects the hit and sends `hit`; the victim applies the damage and sends `dead` when it dies
- One peer per room elected "host"; others connect to it (star)
- Topology mode configurable via `VITE_NETWORK_TOPOLOGY_MODE` env var
- The phone controller has its own PeerJS connection to the game (`_attachPhoneSwordConn` in `bootstrapGameApp.js`; the newest phone connection replaces an older one — `_phoneSwordConn`); TURN servers come from `/api/turn-credentials`. The game's gyro peer (`_createGyroPeer`) stays registered on the signalling server so a phone can reconnect mid-game: it reconnects on `disconnected` (backoff, and at once on `visibilitychange` / `online` / `focus`), is recreated when destroyed, and retries its fixed ID while a stale registration holds it (`_fixedPeerId` = a hash of `profileNameKey` — one phone URL per profile — or, for guests, of a per-browser device ID kept in localStorage + cookie `sq:guestDeviceId`, so the URL survives refreshes); the phone page retries `peer-unavailable` / times out a connection that never opens (`connectToGame` in `public/phone-sword.html`)

### 4. World: maps per mode
`MAPS` in `bootstrapGameApp.js` lists the maps: `islandTown` (`public/mappacks/island_town.mappack`, loaded with `MapLoader` from `src/map/MapLoader.ts` — the map editor's runtime, edited for the game: `userData.mapObject` on placed objects, each placed object wrapped in a group so the `normalisePivot` offset survives (the editor's copy overwrites it, misplacing GLBs whose origin isn't at their bottom-centre), KTX2 loader disposed, fetch errors thrown, model files loaded in parallel; replace it with a new editor copy and re-apply those) (scaled ×1.4 by its `scale` — spawn, `DUEL_LOCATION` / `ARENA_LOCATION` and `STAGE_ROUTES` are in scaled world units; the water sampler un-scales its queries) for Showdown, the tutorial, Town and Multiplayer, and `classic` (`public/glb_map/map.glb`, ×5) for Classic (`mapForMode`). The default map loads at boot; the mode handler (`setModeHandler`) awaits `_setActiveMap(mapForMode(mode))` before starting a mode — it loads a map the first time (cached after), swaps it into the scene, clears the height cache, re-registers the bullet meshes (`registerMapMeshes`) and the spawn centre (`setSpawnCenter`), and a changed map teleports the player to its spawn. Each map's meshes get a `three-mesh-bvh` BVH and one downward-raycast height resolver (`registerTerrainHeightResolver`, `src/environment/terrainHeight.js`) casts against the active map's ground meshes. Everything that needs ground height (`getTerrainHeight`, `getSpawnY`) goes through that resolver. Mappack grass / scatter (instanced) is neither ground nor solid; objects matching a map's `noGround` (trees) and the water meshes (`MAP_NOT_GROUND`: `sea` / `seabed` / `water`) stop bullets but aren't stood on. A pack with a sea level (the island) sets the ground floor `SEA_WADE_DEPTH` under it (`setTerrainFloor`; the flat Rapier ground collider's top follows it too — `_syncPhysicsGround` — so enemies' dynamic bodies can go below y = 0), so characters wade in the sea instead of sinking; `_isOverWater(x, z)` (MapLoader's `sampleMapWaterLevel`, bridges count as dry) makes `_psPathSteepness` steer stage paths away from water (`PS_PATH_WATER_PENALTY`). The first map loaded is the art-style reference (its objects' textures; KTX2 textures are skipped by `artStyle.js`). Multiplayer spots (`DUEL_LOCATION`, `ARENA_LOCATION`) are island town coordinates. A mappack with a sea can carry `floating` GLB props (the island's pirate ship): loaded in `_loadMap` (`_loadFloatingProp`, pack units, origin on the sea surface), stylized, bobbing on the swell (the map's `animate(t)`, called each frame — `FLOAT_*`); they stop bullets and are kept out of the map's height raycast (`noGround` — their masts would be the ground), but one with `deckMaxY` (the ship: 1.5 model units) is ground of its own: `_floatingGroundHeight` (a second, uncached height resolver — it bobs) casts down onto its highest upward-facing surface no higher than `deckMaxY` (the deck — masts / yards / sails ignored), so it is climbed from the water like any wall and stood on. One with a `deck` rect (the ship) can sail: open roam's voyage moves its `holder` (`_voyageUpdate`, see Common Task Locations) and moves it back to its `anchor` afterwards.

### 5. Stages (`_ps*` in `bootstrapGameApp.js`)
Between stages the player is in the **village** (`src/village/villageMode.js`, see Common Task Locations), built around wherever they stand (or at the stage route's first point); the villager stands on the next stage's path and gives a quest for it (accepting starts the stage). A stage with a hand-authored route (`STAGE_ROUTES` in `src/map/stageRoutes.js`, recorded with Settings → Dev → Free Roam) auto-walks along its waypoints (following the line, not touching each point — see Common Task Locations); otherwise it picks the flattest direction from the start (`_psPickPathAngle` — picked when the village opens and passed to `_psStartStage` / `_psBuildStage`). `_psBuildStage` places swordsmen, bombers and coins along it (`_psBuildStage`; from the village they start `PS_VILLAGE_EXIT_DIST` m out so the player walks out first), then auto-walks the player between fights. Enemies (`EnemyPlayer`, `BombThrowerEnemy`) live in the `hordeEnemies` array (historical name).

### 6. GLB Character + IK Arms
Players render `public/models/glb_characters/village_boy.glb` (`glbCharacterConfig.villageBoyUrl`) by default (Showdown lets them pick an unlocked character, Multiplayer battles a roster character); Stage enemies' characters are preloaded in the village (`preloadCharacterModel`) and a queued enemy only spawns once its model is in (`isCharacterModelSettled`), so none fights invisible. EnemyPlayers without a `characterUrl` cycle through `pumpkin.glb`, `antler_guy.glb` (`glbCharacterConfig.antlerGuyUrl`), `frog_man.glb` (`glbCharacterConfig.frogManUrl`), `gemhorn_rigged.glb` (`glbCharacterConfig.url`, the default) `wizard.glb` (`glbCharacterConfig.wizardUrl`) and `tree_creature.glb` (`glbCharacterConfig.treeCreatureUrl`) per spawn (Showdown stages pass one) — all share the Mixamo skeleton, picked with the `url` option of `createGLBCharacterInstance`. No character has shell fur any more (`glbCharacterConfig.fluffy.shells` = 0 — bouncy secondary motion only); per-model fluffy overrides go in `glbCharacterConfig.fluffyByUrl`. Every model is scaled to the same `targetHeight` from its bind-pose bounds; per-model size multipliers go in `glbCharacterConfig.heightScaleByUrl` (tree creature and gemhorn 1.4×, villager (Pemberton) 1.1×, village boy / girl 0.8×; a smaller character also gets a lower player camera — `cameraHeightByUrl` → `characterCameraHeight`, used by `PlayerControls`: village boy 0.9×). `fluffyCharacter.ts` retargets Mixamo FBX clips (walk/run/idle) onto it and adds fur; a moving character plays `Slow Run.fbx` (`runClip`) instead of the walk once its ground speed — measured from its world position in `GLBCharacter.animate` (`_updateSpeed`), so the same for the player, remote players and enemies — passes `glbCharacterConfig.runSpeed` (3 m/s; back to the walk below `runSpeed × runStopRatio`); the clip drives everything **except** the arm chains. Arms are posed each frame by a stretchy two-bone IK (`GLBCharacter.solveArm`) toward invisible floating-hand groups, which are also the weapon attach points (`userData.proceduralHand` markers, preferred by `Weapon._getHandBone`). The GLB and clips face +Z (game forward) — no Y180 needed. Hand labels are mirrored: the `'right'` floating hand is at local +X, i.e. the anatomical left arm. Per-character weapon hold: `glbCharacterConfig.holdByUrl` (`characterHold(url)` → `{ grip, sword }`) scales the floating-hand grip targets in `updateProceduralPlayerRig` / `updateRemotePlayerRig` and the held sword (`FoamSword.update`, `createSwordMesh`) — village boy grips at 0.76 with a 0.8 sword. Which arms the IK drives is per hand (`GLBCharacter.setArmIKHands({ left, right })`, the clip animates the others and restarts to pick them up): the local player with nothing equipped has only the anatomical right arm (`'left'` hand) on the IK, following the phone gyro (`userData.gyroArmQuat` / `gyroArmTime`, set in the phone-sword gyro loop of `bootstrapGameApp.js`, `GYRO_ARM_*` in `playerModel.js`); with no live gyro both arms play the clip. A held pose: `GLBCharacter.playPose(url)` loops a clip over the whole body (IK suspended) until `endPose()` — Town's broom sits the player down with `sitClip` (`Sitting.fbx`). On death, `GLBCharacter.playDeath()` plays `Flying Back Death.fbx` once over the whole body (IK suspended, hands follow the palms) until `revive()`; EnemyPlayer keeps its ragdoll during it, with knockback capped by `DEATH_KNOCKBACK_CAP`. Bomb blasts reuse the clip without dying: `EnemyPlayer.applyBlastKnockback()` (ragdoll + `playDeath()`, `revive()` in `_endRagdoll`, force/cap `BLAST_KNOCKBACK`) and `_blastPlayer()` in `bootstrapGameApp.js` for the local player. `BombThrowerEnemy` uses the same GLB with `armIK: false` (clips drive the arms, no floating hands): `playAction(glbCharacterConfig.throwClip)` plays `Throw.fbx` once and releases the bomb at `THROW_RELEASE_AT` of the clip; between throws a bomb mesh is snapped to the anatomical right palm (`getPalmWorldPosition('left')` — labels are mirrored). Blasts from other throwers play the death clip for `BLAST_STUN_MS`, then `revive()`.

**Mii characters** (`mii1.glb`, later `mii2.glb`… — `isMiiCharacterUrl` matches `/miiN.glb`) have no shoulders, arms or hands. `_buildArm` finds no chains, so `solveArm` leaves the floating hands where the game put them and `getPalmWorldPosition` returns a point beside the body (`palmFallback`, bomber / player bomb). Their hands are drawn by the sword: a Mii holds the Wii sword (`wii_sword.glb`, `SWORD_VARIANTS.wii` in `swordModel.js`) whose instances carry two brown ball hands on the handle. `createSwordModelInstance({ characterUrl })` picks the variant (`swordVariantForCharacter`); `FoamSword` swaps its model when the player's character changes, `EnemyPlayer._buildSword` uses the enemy's character, and `createSwordMesh(characterUrl)` (duel / battle opponents) the opponent's. Miis are Classic only. New Mii: add the GLB, a `glbCharacterConfig` url and add it to `CLASSIC_MII_URLS` (not `MATCH_CHARACTERS`).

**Unrigged characters** (no skeleton — `villager.glb` for now) load through the same `createGLBCharacterInstance`, but `GLBCharacter` gets a `StaticRig` instead of the `FluffyCharacter`: no clips or arm IK, just procedural motion (breathing idle, a walking bob, tipping over on death) and `palmFallback` hands. Rig the GLB (Mixamo skeleton) and it uses the normal path automatically.

### 7. Start screen + tutorial
After login the start screen shows only **Start Game** (runs the tutorial) until the profile has `tutorialCompleted: true`; after that it shows **Tutorial**, **Showdown**, **Town**, **Classic** and **Multiplayer**. Auth resolves as soon as the player is logged in, so the game (and the settings panel) loads behind the start screen; a mode picked before it's ready is queued and started when `setModeHandler` is set (the auto phone-QR popup is deferred until a mode is picked, `autoShowPhoneSwordQr`). When the tutorial finishes, `arcadeOverlay.showStartScreen()` brings the start screen back and the mode handler starts the next mode in the running game. The ⚙️ settings button (`#settings-button`) sits above the start screen, the stage overlay and the Multiplayer lobby (z-index in `styles.css`) and stays visible in every mode (Classic included), hidden until `body.settings-ready` (set once `initSettingsPanel` has run — the default map loads in parallel, `_defaultMapReady`, and the boot never waits on it: the game loop starts without it (the guest's sea start plays meanwhile), a picked mode waits on it in the mode handler, and the player is put on the map's ground at the spawn once it's in unless out on the sea). While a mode is running the settings panel shows a **⬅ Back to Lobby** footer button (`buildFooter` in `settingsPanel.js`) → `appState.returnToLobby` (stops the tutorial via `showdownTutorial.stop()`, exits Multiplayer / Classic, `_resetForMenu`, `showStartScreen`). The tutorial script (`src/tutorial/showdownTutorial.js`) drives enemies through `EnemyPlayer.script` (`passive` / `block` / `windup`) and the bomber's `throwsHeld` / `aimAt`, and touches the game only through `tutorialCtx` in `bootstrapGameApp.js`. While it runs, health never drops (`localHealth` setter), auto-buy is paused (it buys scripted), and tutorial enemies drop no coins and don't count as kills (`_tutorial`).

### 8. UI design system (`styles.css`)
Every screen — start screen, settings / shop / leaderboard (`.settings-shell`), village panels / buy card, phone QR / calibration popups, Multiplayer lobby / battles, tutorial, game over, HUD — shares one dark glass look. The top of `styles.css` holds the **tokens** on `:root` (`--font-ui` Inter / `--font-display` Outfit, loaded from Google Fonts in `index.html`; `--surface*`, `--border*`, `--text*`, gold `--accent*`, status colours, `--radius-*`, `--shadow-*`, `--menu-bg` for full-screen menus, `--scrim` for modal backdrops) and three **shared components** applied by grouped selectors: **panel** (`.ui-panel`, `.arcade-shell`, `.settings-shell`, `.duel-lobby-panel`, phone popups…), **button** — gold primary (`.ui-btn`, `.arcade-button`, `.settings-button`…), glass secondary (`.ui-btn-secondary`, `.arcade-secondary`, …) and ghost (`.ui-btn-ghost`, `.arcade-guest`, back buttons) — and **chip** (`.ui-chip`, settings / leaderboard tabs; selected = `.is-active` / `.active`). New UI: use the `ui-*` classes (or add the new class to the matching group) and the tokens, not new colours / fonts. HUD elements are `--surface-hud` pills without `backdrop-filter` (it would re-blur the 3D view every frame). Classic keeps its Wii-style lettering (stage banner, Ready/Go!, combo) but uses the shared panel/buttons. `public/phone-sword.html` repeats the tokens it needs in its own `:root`.

**Icons in place of emoji:** `public/assets/ui/icons/pixelIcons.js` (a plain script in `index.html` and `public/phone-sword.html`, before the app) maps emoji → a cell of one of two sheets (`SHEETS`): `pixel-icons.webp` (pixel art, lossless — `PIXEL`, [row, column], drawn pixelated) and `painted-icons.webp` (painted, one atlas of the two painted packs — `PAINTED`, wins where both have one: ❗ ❓ 📍, coin, heart, sword, shield, chest, backpack, fire, canoe, gun, house, lock, bullets, the characters' portraits, mood faces, ★ / ☆, the bell…; each pack cut from its dark background — blobs by distance from the background colour, flood-filled from the edges + enclosed holes, edges feathered, packed at 64 px, WebP q80). A MutationObserver wraps every mapped emoji in the DOM in a `<px-icon>` (the emoji stays as its transparent text, so `textContent` is unchanged), so UI code keeps writing plain emoji; canvas text goes through `window.pixelIcons.drawText` / `measureText` (`makeTextSprite` in `villageMode.js` — 3D signs / markers). An emoji with no icon in `ICONS` stays an emoji; to add one, add its cell. Direction: minimal on-screen UI and messages (Town talk = subtitles, no name tags over townsfolk). **Emoji only where it is the content**: item / goods / character icon fields (`emoji:`), counters (coins, bag, bombs, bubbles, kills, mana), prices next to a number, ❗ / ❓ / 📍 markers, ★ stars and icon-only buttons; button labels, feed lines, toasts, banners, speech and tutorial text are plain words ("+3 coins", "Shop", "Get out"). Button labels quoted elsewhere in this guide may still show the emoji they used to carry.

### 9. PIN Auth
No OAuth. Player registers with name + numeric PIN. PIN is `SALT + SHA-256` hashed client-side via Web Crypto, stored in Firebase. Hash cached in cookie for auto-login.

**Guest play:** the app opens as a guest (`startAsGuest` in `createArcadeOverlay`) unless a signed-in player is remembered (stored PIN → auto-login). The start screen's "Sign In" / "Switch user" button (`data-arcade-switch`, kept visible when returning from any mode via `showStartScreen`) reloads the page with the `sq:showLogin` sessionStorage flag, which opens the login form instead; "Play Without Signing In" there (`data-arcade-guest`) goes back to guest. Guest play skips auth with a random name (`randomGuestName`) and an in-memory profile (`buildGuestProfile`, all modes offered). `initCore` then has `isGuest` true and `profileNameKey` null — every profile save (`saveStats*` wrappers, stage/characters/tutorial/phoneSwordStats, localStorage `ps_*` keys) is skipped when the key is null, `playerName` isn't stored, and renaming only changes the session name. Guard new profile writes on `profileNameKey`.
